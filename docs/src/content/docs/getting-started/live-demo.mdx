The cross sections are the published fits that come with the package: the elastic momentum
transfer of Park et al. (2022) and the vibrational and electronic excitation and the ionization
of Janev, Reiter and Samm (2003). On this plot a Maxwellian is a straight line.

- At a fraction of a townsend the electrons gain little energy between collisions and stay
  close to the gas temperature: the distribution is nearly a Maxwellian.
- From a few to some tens of townsends the vibrational excitation (thresholds 0.5 and 1 eV)
  takes most of the power the field gives the electrons. It cuts the distribution off above a
  few eV, orders of magnitude below the Maxwellian. Switch it off and the mean energy rises
  two- to threefold.
- Around 100 Td the electronic excitations (above 8 eV) and the ionization deplete the tail
  above about 20 eV.

The same in a script:

```python
import plasmacoll
from plasmacoll.constants import ATOMIC_MASS, kelvin_to_ev
from plasmacoll.hydrogen_elastic import ELASTIC_FITS
from plasmacoll.janev import JANEV_FITS

h2 = plasmacoll.NeutralBackground("H2", 1e22, kelvin_to_ev(300.0), 2.016 * ATOMIC_MASS)
processes = [
    plasmacoll.CollisionProcess("elastic", "H2", ELASTIC_FITS["H2"].cross_section()),
    *(
        plasmacoll.CollisionProcess(
            "excitation", "H2", JANEV_FITS[key].cross_section(), name=key
        )
        for key in ("e_h2_vibrational_1", "e_h2_vibrational_2", "e_h2_triplet_b")
    ),
    plasmacoll.CollisionProcess(
        "ionization",
        "H2",
        JANEV_FITS["e_h2_ionization"].cross_section(),
        name="ionization",
        products={"electron": "e", "ion": "H2+"},
    ),
]
solution = plasmacoll.solve_eedf(processes, [h2], 30.0)  # E/N in Td
print(solution.mean_energy, solution.rate_coefficients["ionization"])
```

See the [two-term Boltzmann example](/plasmacoll/examples/07-two-term-boltzmann/) for the
method and its checks.
